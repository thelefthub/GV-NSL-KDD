\begin{abstract}
  Motivated by the limitations of traditional rule-based intrusion detection systems and the increasing complexity of modern cyber attacks, 
  this study evaluates Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Neural Networks for network intrusion detection on the NSL-KDD benchmark dataset in both binary and multi-class classification settings. 
  A unified preprocessing pipeline is applied to all models, and severe class imbalance is addressed through class-weighted learning. Model performance is assessed using accuracy, macro-averaged 
  metrics, and attack-aware evaluation measures, including per-class recall, confusion-matrix analysis, and attack-only macro recall that 
  excludes the dominant Normal class. In the binary setting, all models achieve relatively high test accuracy (up to 0.778) and strong ranking performance, 
  as reflected by ROC–AUC values above 0.89, but show a substantial performance drop from validation to test data, indicating limited generalization to unseen attack types. 
  In the multi-class setting, aggregate metrics mask major weaknesses in minority attack detection. Random Forest achieves near-zero recall for R2L and U2R attacks, 
  while Logistic Regression improves minority recall at the cost of strong over-generalization. Neural Networks provide the most balanced attack detection, 
  substantially improving U2R recall but introducing additional confusion among non-dominant classes. Overall, the results show that while, Random Forest offers the 
  most practical balance for general intrusion detection, effective multi-class intrusion detection is limited primarily by 
  data characteristics rather than model choice. This finding highlights the importance of attack-aware evaluation and data-centric strategies such as hierarchical detection pipelines, 
  targeted handling of rare attack classes, and validation on distribution-shifted test data.
\end{abstract}
